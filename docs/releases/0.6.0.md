TabStudio 0.6 brings desktop mode: connect a monitor, and the whole IDE moves to the big screen while the tablet becomes its touch companion.

## What's new in 0.6

- **Desktop mode.**
  - Connect a monitor over USB-C or HDMI, or a wireless display, and TabStudio offers **Use desktop mode**. Choose **Always** to switch on whenever a display connects; the status bar's monitor button and View > Desktop Mode turn it on and off.
  - The whole IDE moves as it is: the same documents, caret, scroll, undo, panels and running debugger. Its size is automatic, or 100 to 200%.
  - A keyboard connected to the tablet types on the big screen, with every shortcut. A mouse or touchpad on the tablet drives TabStudio's pointer there: click, drag and scroll; on a touchpad, two fingers scroll and a tap clicks. Touch the tablet to use it directly again.
  - The tablet becomes a companion:
    - big Start, Run and Build buttons, and the debugger's Continue, Step Over, Step Into, Step Out, Restart and Stop;
    - a searchable Toolbox of Avalonia controls that inserts the one you tap at the caret;
    - a Properties panel that edits the XAML element at the caret, with dropdowns for choices, reset and undo;
    - without a keyboard, a typing bar that sends the on-screen keyboard to the editor on the big screen, with Tab, arrow and Esc keys.
  - The floating touch buttons stay on the tablet. Unplugging the display, or **Back to the tablet**, brings everything back.

## Install

1. Download `TabStudio-0.6.0.apk` below on your tablet.
2. Open it and allow installing apps from your browser or file manager when Android asks.

Requires Android 9 or later on 64-bit or 32-bit ARM, with 3 GB of memory or more (2 GB with TabStudio Link). It installs as an update over 0.5.

## Verified

Every commit runs these checks in CI:

- the unit tests of every library;
- headless UI tests that type, tap and press keys in the real IDE, with screenshots, including desktop mode on a simulated display: the move and the return, the companion, the Toolbox and Properties, the pointer and the typing bar;
- an APK build with signature verification;
- the app on an Android tablet emulator. There it runs the sample and answers its question, debugs it to a breakpoint, debugs it through a TabStudio Link host, serves a new ASP.NET Core app, shows a new Avalonia app, and previews the app's view in the XAML designer. On a simulated second display it then moves the IDE there, inserts a control from the Toolbox, sets a property, types on the big screen from the tablet's keyboard, clicks and scrolls with a captured mouse, and brings the IDE back.

This release was published by CI only after its own build passed all of them.
